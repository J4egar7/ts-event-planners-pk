Six briefs we're asked for most

Corporate work is judged on things guests never mention: registration that moves, sessions that start on the minute, lunch that's ready when it was promised, and a room that can absorb a change of plan at four in the afternoon.

Conferences & seminars

Registration desks, seating layouts, speaker support and session changeovers that happen in the tea break rather than during the talk.

AGMs & board meetings

Formal seating, papers and signage in place before the directors arrive, and a room that stays quiet for the minutes.

Product launches

The reveal is seconds long. We rehearse the cue — lights, screens, the walk-on — until it lands the same way twice.

Award nights & annual dinners

Trophies in order, names pronounced correctly, winners finding the stage without a search party, and dinner served hot.

Government & institutional events

Protocol seating, security and access coordination, and a programme that accommodates a late arrival without collapsing.

Office functions & team days

In-house celebrations, farewells and family days where the venue, food and timing all have to fit around a working day.

01 · The brief

Built backwards from the start time

Every corporate event has a fixed point — the ribbon, the keynote, the reveal, the dinner call. We build the schedule backwards from it, then test it against load-in access, vendor arrival times and how long the room actually takes to turn around.

  • Run-of-show written to the minute, shared with your team
  • Load-in and setup windows agreed with the venue in writing
  • Contingency for the late flight, the missing shipment and the changed speaker
  • One coordinator on site as your point of contact
02 · The room

Seats, sightlines and the back row

A conference room is a working space. The screen has to be legible from the last row, the aisles have to let people move without crossing the camera, and the registration desk has to be somewhere people find before they queue.

  • Theatre, classroom, cabaret and boardroom layouts
  • Stage, podium and panel setups sized to the session
  • Signage, branding and delegate materials placed on arrival
  • AV, sound and screen suppliers coordinated with your team
03 · Hospitality

Tea, lunch and the seated dinner

Catering at a corporate event is a logistics problem before it's a menu. Three hundred people with a thirty-minute break need a service plan, not just good food.

  • Tea and coffee service timed to the programme
  • Working lunches and buffet lines sized to the break
  • Seated dinners with a table plan and place cards
  • Dietary requirements handled without a public conversation

Before the meeting

What we need from you

Date & hours

The day and the exact window you have the room — including setup access the evening before, if the venue allows.

Headcount

Delegates, speakers, staff and VIPs, plus whether the room needs to change layout during the day.

Programme

Sessions, speakers, breaks, the reveal or the awards — and which parts cannot move.

Approvals

Who signs off on the venue, the menu and the final count, and by what date. It saves a week at the end.
